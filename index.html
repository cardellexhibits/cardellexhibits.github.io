Services

Four ways we keep a station running.

Each one ends with something in your hand: a list, a working station, a spare, or the notes.

01

Survey

We come to the learning center, play each interactive, and open the cabinet if it is meant to open. Then we write down what is in it and what is failing.

You get

  • One half-day on site, every station played by hand.
  • A punch list in five days: each station named, what is wrong, one clear next step.
  • A note on anything that belongs with another trade, so nothing surprises you later.
Email about a survey
02

Break/fix

The station already on the floor. We diagnose it and repair it so the same show runs again.

You get

  • Someone on site who already knows this kind of cabinet.
  • Cables, memory cards, power supplies, and buttons replaced, so the exhibit stays itself.
  • A straight answer if the old player is dead. That is a conversion, and we say so before we start.
Email about break/fix
03

Conversion

The player or controller is dead. We take it out and put in a new one that plays the same media, uses the same buttons, and sits in the same cabinet.

You get

  • The station working again, playing the same show.
  • A spare memory card you can swap yourself.
  • A one-page operator card in plain English: how to power it, how to swap the spare, who to email.
Email about a conversion
04

Care

A one-year agreement for the stations you name. We come back on a schedule, play them, and tell you what is wearing out before Saturday does.

You get

  • A named list of stations, in writing, for the year.
  • Periodic checks where we play each one, not just power it on.
  • Someone who already has the spare and the notes. Parts and conversions are quoted first.
Email about care

Why Cardell

Three habits we keep on every job.

They are why a station we touch is easier to live with the next year, not harder.

We keep the exhibit.

The cabinet, the buttons, and the show your visitors know stay right where they are. We work behind them.

We document the work.

Every visit ends with notes: what we found, what we changed, what to watch. You can hand them to the next person.

We leave a spare and an operator card.

One page in plain English, plus the spare that fits. Your staff can keep the station running without us in the building.

We stay in the cabinet.

We service the station already in the cabinet: the player, the buttons, the show. New galleries, new videos, and TVs on the wall belong with a different shop, and our punch list will say so.
